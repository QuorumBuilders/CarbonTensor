# Section Copy: AWD Explainer & Smallholder Aggregation

### Section Pill
Agronomic Method

### Headline (2 Lines Max)
Aerating paddy soil stops methane formation  
without reducing rice yield.

### Subtitle (3 Lines Max)
Continuous flooding keeps paddy soil in an oxygen-depleted state where microbes release methane gas. Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) breaks this cycle through planned field drainage down to the root zone.

### Comparison Cards

#### Card 1: Continuous Flooding (Baseline)
* **Water Level:** `+5.0 cm` (Standing Water)
* **Biological Condition:** Anaerobic decomposition. Stagnant floodwater prevents atmospheric oxygen from reaching topsoil organic matter, triggering constant methane ($\text{CH}_4$) gas release.
* **Farmer Reality:** Heavy pumping fuel expenditure, standing water weed management, zero carbon verification entitlement.

#### Card 2: Safe AWD Aeration (Verified Practice)
* **Water Level:** `-15.0 cm` (Subsurface Aeration)
* **Biological Condition:** Oxygenated root zone. The water table recedes through natural percolation and evaporation to $-15\text{ cm}$ below ground, halting methanogenesis while protecting root moisture.
* **Farmer Reality:** Up to 30% reduction in irrigation water and diesel pump fuel, stronger tillering, and qualified carbon dividend eligibility.

### Aggregation Note (The Cooperative Solution)
* **Headline:** Cooperative aggregation makes smallholder audits viable.
* **Body:** The typical rice parcel in Northern Nigeria is 1 to 2 hectares. Individual verification site visits cost thousands of dollars per farm, making standalone carbon certification impossible. By aggregating hundreds of contiguous plots under unified cooperative governance, CarbonTensor bundles acreage into institutional cohorts that meet international registry scale.
